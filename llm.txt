THE USER'S INTENT
Keep the familiar Ragnarok MMO feeling: exploration, meaningful progression,
classes, equipment, encounters and social places. Present an original, coherent
modern 3D world. Use God of War for grounded construction, material and lighting
discipline; Warcraft for readable silhouettes, landmarks and expressive fantasy.
AAA is a craft target. Do not claim AAA parity, a large team, or production scale
because a prompt, model generation, build, test suite or polygon count succeeded.

MANDATORY WORKING RULES
1. Finish one excellent playable Sunmeadow region before extending the lava region.
   Keep two combat clearings, compact connections, useful discoveries and a return
   route. Preserve metre scale and the character's clearance.
2. Start from a reference breakdown and a frozen player-camera baseline. Lock the
   shared palette, silhouettes, construction language and material scale.
3. First make the route physically playable: floors, stairs, slopes, bridges,
   doorways, colliders and server movement must agree. No cosmetic height fix
   that leaves the actual player beneath the floor or passing through a wall.
4. Prefer proven free assets and tools when their output fits the art direction.
   Trees normally start from a procedural tree generator or a suitable licensed
   kit, followed by art direction, foliage/LOD/wind work and engine verification.
   Do not replace mature tools with a rushed home-made generator.
   Tree decision (owner asked 2026-10-01 and again 2026-10-02; CC0 downloads are
   approved): docs/reviews/2026-10-02-trees-free-assets-decision.md. Route A is the
   Quaternius Stylized Nature MegaKit (CC0); route B is EZ-Tree v2 or the pinned
   Modular Tree, with the stylized clump recipe. Home-made tree generators, Tripo
   trees and photographic leaves on stylized trees are no longer allowed. Report
   any rejected route to the owner the same day.
5. Use image generation for concept decisions and appropriate source artwork.
   A beautiful generated image is not a playable-scene result. Image-to-3D output
   still needs geometry, UV, material, rig and multi-angle inspection.
6. Work in a two-role loop: Concept/Art Direction -> Build/Technical Art -> actual
   Babylon capture -> evidence-based critique -> repair. Use real tool capacity;
   do not invent parallel agents or describe a self-review as independent review.
7. Separate technical, visual, gameplay and device-performance verdicts. Failure
   in one cannot be offset by success in another. Unknown means unverified.
8. Review full-player, side, close and elevated views, plus animation and traversal.
   Keep the camera, light, build, preset and asset revision fixed for comparisons.
9. Preserve clean silhouettes and readable combat. Put detail into useful forms,
   normal/roughness maps and controlled dressing. More clutter, bloom, triangles,
   unique textures or an 8K source do not establish better quality.
10. Use the hybrid pipeline: authored GLB/material/audio files, bounded shaders,
    particles and procedural systems, and HTML/Svelte UI. Measure simultaneous
    rendering, texture residency, decode cost and frame times separately from bytes.
11. Keep source art immutable, versions reproducible, and asset licences/provenance
    recorded. A local reference folder is not evidence that its contents are CC0.
    - Quaternius packs released after 2026-08-28 use Quaternius's own "QAL"
      licence, not CC0. QAL allows use in the game but not redistribution of the
      assets.
    - Older Quaternius copies (Stylized Nature, Fantasy Props, Medieval Village)
      carry CC0 in their own licence files.
    - Before using any new Quaternius zip, read the licence file inside it. QAL
      packs, such as the Bestiary kit, need the owner's approval.
    - RaGEZONE content is leaked or proprietary. Never use it; open-source
      emulators found there are study-only.
    - Source: docs/reviews/2026-10-02-free-asset-scout.md.
12. Report the strongest remaining defects plainly. Repair them before expanding
    scope; when a method repeatedly fails, change the method and explain why.
13. Prefer reviewed Blender Python recipes for repeatable kit assembly, exact
    dimensions, validation, LODs, baking, export and review renders. Keep existing
    generators and hand-authored detail where they produce better results. A long
    script is not a substitute for good forms or actual engine inspection.
14. Treat Tripo DCC Bridge as an optional ingestion route. Read the Blender/Tripo
    workflow before use; verify the receiver and received asset, then preserve an
    immutable source. Bridge success is not rig, material or game-readiness proof.

FIRST-PASS QUALITY — NO RE-PROMPTING (OWNER RULE, 2026-10-01)
The owner had to prompt 4-5 times before work was good enough. Every agent
(Codex threads and workers, froggy, Claude) must reach the quality bar in its
first report. "Best", "AAA", "many details" and "not kid models" are standing
requirements; the owner should never have to repeat them in a prompt.
1. Before building, extract every explicit and implied requirement from the
   request and from the checklists below. Write a short build spec covering:
   - the target reference;
   - the numbers (sizes, budgets, timings, palette);
   - the definition of done;
   - the evidence you will capture.
   Keep the spec in the task report.
2. For any visual deliverable, run build -> capture -> critique against the
   spec -> repair at least 3 times before reporting. Each pass fixes the 3-5
   most visible defects first. Compare before/after images from the same camera.
3. When two techniques fit, choose the better player-visible result unless its
   measured cost breaks the budget. That means:
   - lit and normal-mapped over flat;
   - blended over popping;
   - layered materials over single colours;
   - authored forms over primitives;
   - a real engine capture over a mock-up or a Blender-only render.
4. Finish the edges in the same task. Unfinished edges are defects, not
   follow-ups. The edges are:
   - both renderers (WebGPU and WebGL2);
   - disposal and cleanup;
   - tests;
   - LODs, colliders and receipts;
   - naming and docs;
   - a review page or capture.
5. If a request is ambiguous, pick the reading that is better for the player,
   state the assumption in one line and continue. Ask only for approvals
   (installs, paid jobs, downloads, publishing) or for owner-only choices.
6. Never report "done" with a known major defect. Report what passed, with its
   evidence, what is still weak, and the next repair you recommend.

LOOK TARGET (OWNER REFERENCES, 2026-10-01)
A stylised, hand-painted fantasy MMO with Warcraft-like readability, combined
with the Ragnarok warmth and God of War construction discipline described above.
- Forms: chunky, confident proportions. Key features are oversized (hands,
  weapons, shoulder pads, monster heads). Shapes are bevelled, thick and readable.
- Colour: saturated but harmonious. A warm sun key with a soft blue sky fill.
  Shared palette: warm stone, light plaster, dark timber, blue slate.
- Textures: the style lives in the albedo. Paint into it:
  - large top-light gradients;
  - occlusion in crevices;
  - crisp edge highlights;
  - hue variation.
  Normal maps stay supportive. Roughness varies across a surface but is never
  noisy.
- Combat: big, timed spell shapes (ground swirls, rising motes, staff glow),
  instant hit flashes, and nameplates and health bars that read at 13 m.
- World: lived-in clusters placed with purpose, never in rows. Examples: carts,
  haystacks, crates, barrels, tents, palisades, broadleaf trees, mushrooms and
  painted rocks.
- UI: ornate gold and bronze frames, a round minimap, unit frames, an action bar
  with crest bookends, a quest tracker and chat panels. Thai text stays legible.
- Originality: no Blizzard or WoW characters, crests, gryphon bookends, icons,
  logos or fonts. Xexoria has its own crest beast and icon language.

CRAFT CHECKLISTS — APPLY EVERY RELEVANT LINE
Meshes
- The silhouette reads at 13 m in greyscale. Proportions follow the approved
  concept sheet, not primitives. Raw boxes, cylinders or scaled spheres are never
  finished forms.
- Construction logic: every part is supported, joined and has thickness. Door and
  window frames have depth, beams rest on supports, roofs have eaves and ridge
  caps.
- Chamfer hard edges with weighted normals: about 2-4 cm on architecture and
  0.5-1.5 cm on props. Bake high-poly detail into the normal and AO maps.
- Geometry:
  - metre scale;
  - pivot at the ground contact;
  - manifold;
  - no coplanar overlaps (z-fighting);
  - edge flow at joints that deform.
- Every placed asset ships with LOD0-2, a collider proxy and, where needed, a
  shadow proxy. Count triangles after export. Budgets are in section 2 of the
  froggy art plan.

Textures and materials
- Build the albedo in layers:
  1. base colour;
  2. large top-light gradient;
  3. painted cavity occlusion;
  4. edge highlights;
  5. hue and value variation;
  6. story marks (moss, soot, wear).
- No flat untextured fills except deliberate accents. Keep albedo values in a
  believable range: never pure black or pure white.
- Texel density, consistent within a view:
  - about 512 px/m for hero props and the key parts of characters;
  - about 256 px/m for general architecture.
- Break up tiling with macro variation, trim sheets, decals and vertex colour.
  Judge repeats from the player camera, not only in close-up.
- After KTX2 compression, check again:
  - colour space (albedo sRGB, data maps linear);
  - normal Y direction;
  - ORM channels;
  - alpha edges and mipmaps.

Lighting, shadows and post
- The sun key, sky fill and IBL work as one set. Forms read in light and in
  shade.
- Contact shadows ground every object.
- No shadow acne, peter-panning or shimmering while the camera moves. Tune the
  cascades for the 13 m camera.
- Emissives and bloom never clip lamps, crystals, water or portals to white.
- Review dawn, noon, dusk and night, all from the one shared world clock.

Sky, clouds and fog
- The sky gradient and the sun glow agree with the sun direction and the fog
  colour.
- Clouds are lit and layered and they drift. Never ball clouds or evenly spaced
  puffs.
- Fog creates depth (aerial perspective). It never washes the scene flat.

Foliage, grass and nature
- Grass is clumped, colour-varied and moved by the wind. Never a carpet or a
  flat tint.
- Trees have taper, root flare, a branch hierarchy and gaps in the crown.
  Leaves are cards or clusters with correct alpha, back faces and mipmaps, and
  controlled overdraw.
- Distant trees and rocks become impostor cards or HLOD, with the same palette
  and fog. Check the swap distance for popping.
  - Impostor baker: assets/blender/tools/impostor_baker.py
  - Runtime: apps/client/src/impostors.ts

Water and snow
- Water has a flow direction, depth tint, Fresnel, foam where it meets objects,
  and moving normals. Water never glows, and bloom never sits on water.
- Snow sits on upward-facing surfaces, drifts against obstacles and keeps its
  material detail. It shows footprints and wet edges where the game supports
  them.

VFX, smoke and fire
- Every effect shows an event and a direction: anticipation -> impact ->
  dissipation. Give windup, active and fade timings in milliseconds that match
  the combat contract.
- Each school has core and edge colours. Effects read over grass, stone and snow,
  and at night. They are scaled for the 13 m camera, pooled, and capped in
  lifetime and count.
- Smoke and fire use flipbooks, simulated in Blender or hand-painted, with soft
  particles and wind drift. They rise, spread and fade believably, and never
  hide combat.

UI
- One ornate gold and bronze frame language: consistent bevels, gems and corner
  pieces. Icons share one painted style at 128 px.
- Test at 1920x1080 and 844x390, by day and by night, with long Thai strings.
  Nothing may overlap the joystick, skills, chat or interaction prompts.
- Never show a menu or system as working when it is not built.

Levels and dungeons
- The blockout is walkable from the start: floors, stairs, slopes, colliders and
  server movement agree. Test it with the real game camera from the first
  blockout.
- Interiors keep the 13 m camera boom clear, or rely on camera collision. Measure
  the boom in every room.
- Each space has one clear landmark, a readable route and a way back.
